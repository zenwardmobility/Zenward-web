2. RESOURCE ARCHITECTURE
==================================================
Typed, version-controlled data in three layers, with no CMS, MDX or database:

- src/lib/resources/slugs.ts: the six slugs and resourcePath(). This is the only
  Resource module the Zenny bundle imports.
- src/lib/resources/catalog.ts: ResourceMeta for each Resource: slug, path, title,
  description, category, audience, kind (guide/checklist/tool), publishedDate,
  updatedDate, featured, relatedServices (the first is the CTA service),
  relatedResources, seoTitle, seoDescription and cardAction. Helpers: getResource,
  resourcesForService, primaryServiceFor.
- src/lib/resources/content.ts: the article bodies as typed blocks (p, h2, list,
  checklist, note), plus intro, bridge and the tool items. It is imported only by
  server components.
- src/lib/resources/schema.ts: Article JSON-LD builder.

All four modules use relative imports, so node tests can load them.

Routing: src/app/resources/page.tsx (hub) and src/app/resources/[slug]/page.tsx
(generateStaticParams; dynamicParams = false, so an unknown slug returns 404). All seven
pages are statically prerendered.

5. CONTENT QUALITY STRATEGY
==================================================
- The content educates first. Each Resource answers the planning question (what to share,
  timing, return trips, access and what happens after a request) before it mentions Zenward.
- Short paragraphs and descriptive H2s. Pages with 4 or more H2s get an "On this page"
  contents list.
- No cannibalisation of service pages. Service pages describe what Zenward offers;
  Resources explain how to plan. Titles and H1s differ from the service pages, and the two
  cross-link.
- Medical boundary: transportation information only. There is no diagnosis, medication or
  treatment guidance. The dialysis guide includes a note: "for questions about treatment,
  speak with your care team". The discharge timing copy had a medication mention, which I
  removed.
