2. UX / CONTENT REASONING
======================================================================
Audit finding: the homepage explained "what you can count on" and "getting to
care should feel more certain", but nowhere said what a medical trip involves
beyond pickup-to-destination. /services only listed services. The 03A pages
explain each service, so the missing piece was a short cross-service statement.

Placement: homepage, between "Patients & Families" (white) and "For Healthcare
Providers" (navy), as a MIST section. Reasons:
  - It comes after the trust and audience content has established Zenward, and
    before the provider section and the service list, so it frames both.
  - It is audience-neutral (passengers, families, caregivers, care teams), so
    sitting between the patient and provider sections suits it.
  - Tone rhythm stays intact: mist > white > mist > white > navy > white > mist.
  - No new page: there is no information-architecture reason for one, and the
    concept is one idea, not a destination.

Shape: an editorial ruled list, not a card grid. Left: eyebrow, headline,
supporting paragraph. Right: four principles separated by hairlines, no icons,
no boxes, no numbering (so it doesn't read as steps or product features).
Below: one plain sentence ("Submitting a request is not a confirmed booking...")
next to the Request Transportation + Call buttons.

Tone: describes what a medical trip involves and what Zenward does about it. No
competitor is named or compared; no superiority, capability or clinical claim.
Headline "Transportation planned around care, not just a pickup" uses the brief's
core idea; "not just a pickup" states what the service adds without comparing to
anyone.

Service pages: the concept is not duplicated. Each 03A page already has an
"All services" link to /services in its hero, and the new homepage section links
to the recurring-care page. /services gets one supporting sentence. No large
block was added to any service page.
